Why Visit Gilgit?

Gilgit is your doorway to the Karakoram & Greater Himalaya — ideal for multi-day treks, scenic road trips on the Karakoram Highway, cultural exchanges and photography.

Top Highlights

  • Hunza Valley: Baltit & Altit forts, apricot orchards and stunning viewpoints.
  • Attabad Lake: Turquoise waters — boating & photo stops.
  • Deosai Plains: High-altitude plateau with wildflowers and wildlife.
  • Passu Cones & Batura Glacier: Iconic Karakoram scenery.

Suggested 5-Day Itinerary

  1. Day 1: Arrive Gilgit — rest & local bazaar walk.
  2. Day 2: Drive to Hunza — Baltit Fort & Karimabad exploration.
  3. Day 3: Attabad Lake & Passu viewpoint day trip.
  4. Day 4: Deosai or Astore day excursion (seasonal).
  5. Day 5: Return to Gilgit & departure.

Adjust days for trekking routes or travel time — many drives are scenic but can be slow.

Practical Information

Getting there

Flights from Islamabad to Gilgit or long road journey via Karakoram Highway. Book flights early due to limited seats.

Accommodation

Range from guesthouses and homestays in Hunza to hotels in Gilgit. Peak season: July–Sept; book in advance.

Health & Altitude

Acclimatise slowly, stay hydrated and avoid strenuous activity on arrival. Seek local advice for high-altitude treks.

Money & Connectivity

ATMs limited in smaller towns — carry cash. Mobile connectivity is patchy in valleys; pre-download maps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most tourists do not require special permits for main valleys like Hunza or Skardu, but some treks near borders may need NOC (No Objection Certificates). Always confirm with local authorities or your tour operator.

Gilgit-Baltistan is generally safe and welcoming. However, mountain roads can be risky during rain or snow, so always travel with an experienced driver and check conditions beforehand.

The best time is from May to October when the weather is pleasant, mountain passes are open, and fruit orchards are in full bloom.

You can reach Gilgit either by a short one-hour flight from Islamabad (weather permitting) or a scenic 16-hour drive via the Karakoram Highway.

Local dishes like Chapshuro, Mamtu, and Buckwheat bread are popular. Most tourist areas also serve Pakistani and international cuisine.

Major towns like Gilgit and Hunza have 4G coverage, but remote valleys may have limited or no connectivity. Carry cash as digital payments are not common everywhere.

Local tip

Try apricot jam from Hunza — a local specialty. Carry small change for roadside stalls and tea houses.

Fact about Gilgit Baltistan

It is home to some of the world's highest mountain ranges, including the Karakoram, the western Himalayas, the Pamir, and the Hindu Kush. The region contains five of the world's 14 "eight-thousanders" (peaks over 8,000 meters)
