Why Visit Gilgit?

Gilgit is your doorway to the Karakoram & Greater Himalaya — ideal for multi-day treks, scenic road trips, cultural exchanges and photography.

Top Highlights

  • Hunza Valley: Baltit & Altit forts, apricot orchards and stunning viewpoints.
  • Attabad Lake: Turquoise waters — boating & photo stops.
  • Deosai Plains: High-altitude plateau with wildflowers and wildlife.
  • Passu Cones & Batura Glacier: Iconic Karakoram scenery.

Suggested 5-Day Itinerary

  1. Day 1: Arrive Gilgit — rest & local bazaar walk.
  2. Day 2: Drive to Hunza — Baltit Fort & Karimabad exploration.
  3. Day 3: Attabad Lake & Passu viewpoint day trip.
  4. Day 4: Deosai or Astore day excursion (seasonal).
  5. Day 5: Return to Gilgit & departure.

Adjust days for trekking routes or travel time — many drives are scenic but can be slow.

Practical Information Quick tips

Getting there

Fly Islamabad → Gilgit for fastest access (limited seats). Scenic road option: Karakoram Highway — allow extra travel time for stops and occasional delays.

Tip: book flights 4–6 weeks ahead for peak season.
Accommodation

From homestays in Hunza to mid-range hotels in Gilgit. Peak season (Jul–Sep) fills quickly — reserve early if you have fixed dates.

Pro-tip: homestays offer local meals and warm hospitality.
Health & altitude

Acclimatise slowly: rest on arrival and avoid heavy exertion for 24–48 hrs. Stay hydrated and carry basic altitude meds if you're prone to AMS.

If symptoms persist, descend and seek local medical help.
Money & connectivity

ATMs are in larger towns but rare in small villages — carry cash for excursions. Mobile signal is patchy in remote valleys; download offline maps before heading out.

Local prepaid SIMs improve coverage in towns.
  • Packing checklist
  • Warm layers & waterproof jacket
  • Good hiking shoes & socks
  • Sunscreen, sunglasses, hat
  • Reusable water bottle, snacks & first-aid
  • Cash for remote areas / small purchases
Safety note
Mountain weather can change rapidly — check local forecasts and road conditions. For high-altitude treks hire a local guide, share your itinerary, and carry a charged phone / power bank.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most tourists do not require special permits for main valleys like Hunza or Skardu, but some treks near borders may need NOC (No Objection Certificates). Always confirm with local authorities or your tour operator.

Gilgit-Baltistan is generally safe and welcoming. However, mountain roads can be risky during rain or snow, so always travel with an experienced driver and check conditions beforehand.

The best months are May to October when the weather is mild, roads are mostly clear, and high-altitude trekking is safer. Winter months can be very cold and many roads may be closed due to snow.

Options range from guesthouses and homestays in Hunza and Skardu to mid-range hotels in Gilgit. Booking in advance is recommended during peak season (July–September).

You can fly from Islamabad to Gilgit (most convenient) or travel via road on the Karakoram Highway. Road trips are scenic but can be long and bumpy.

Yes, local food is generally safe. Stick to freshly cooked meals, avoid raw street food if unsure, and drink bottled or boiled water to prevent stomach issues.

Mobile networks cover most towns but can be patchy in remote valleys. Internet is slower outside Gilgit city; consider offline maps and guides for trekking.

Guided tours are recommended for trekking, off-road excursions, or visits near border areas. For main tourist routes, you can travel independently but must be cautious on mountain roads.

Warm clothing, comfortable trekking shoes, sunscreen, sunglasses, reusable water bottle, first aid kit, and cash for remote areas. For summer, light layers are essential; for winter, heavy jackets and thermals.

Packing Checklist

Altitude-friendly essentials for Gilgit-Baltistan:

  • Warm layers (jackets, fleece, thermals)
  • Comfortable trekking shoes / boots
  • Hiking socks & gloves
  • Sunscreen, sunglasses, and hat
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Basic first aid kit (bandages, painkillers, altitude meds)
  • Flashlight / headlamp with extra batteries
  • Snacks / energy bars for treks
  • Camera / smartphone for photography
  • Cash for small towns & remote areas
Local tip

Try apricot jam from Hunza — a local specialty. Carry small change for roadside stalls and tea houses.

Fact about Gilgit Baltistan

It is home to some of the world's highest mountain ranges, including the Karakoram, the western Himalayas, the Pamir and the Hindu Kush. The region contains five of the world's 14 "eight-thousanders" (peaks over 8,000 meters).
